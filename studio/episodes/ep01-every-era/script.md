# EP01 — How Long Would You Survive in Every Era of Earth?

Status: DRAFT v1 — waiting for owner approval (do not produce audio/animation yet)
Target: ~12 min · ~1,850 words · narrator 150–155 wpm
Format: countdown #12 (worst) → #1 (best). On-screen: survival TIMER (top-right), TIER BOARD that fills up between eras, era label + "millions of years ago".

Legend: **[V]** visual / animation note · **[SFX]** sound · **NARRATOR** voice-over · **DAVE** short lines (pitched TTS voice or speech-bubble text only)

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:45)

[V] Black screen. Phone alarm. Dave wakes up on bare black rock, red sky, hoodie smoking slightly. Phone: "No signal".

NARRATOR: This is Dave. Dave has a hoodie, a phone with seventy-two percent battery, and absolutely no survival skills.

DAVE: Hello? …Is this Ohio?

NARRATOR: It is not Ohio. A broken time machine is about to drop Dave into twelve different eras of Earth's history. Some will kill him in seconds. One might actually let him live.

[V] Timer appears top-right: 00:00. Tier board slides in: 12 empty slots.

NARRATOR: The rules: Dave lands somewhere typical for that era, near fresh water when there is any, and never at the exact second of a disaster… unless the disaster *is* the era. We'll count down from the worst place Dave could possibly land, to the best. And the number one spot is not the one you're thinking of.

[SFX] Time machine glitch. Title card: **DAVE VS HISTORY**.

---

## #12 — THE HADEAN · 4.5 billion years ago (0:45–1:35)

[V] Molten orange landscape, meteors streaking, steam everywhere. Dave lands, sneaker instantly melts.

NARRATOR: Number twelve. The Hadean, the very first chapter of Earth. And it's named after Hell for a reason.

NARRATOR: The young planet is still being pounded by space rocks. Much of its surface is hot enough to be partly molten, and the air is a choking mix of volcanic gases, with essentially no oxygen to breathe.

[V] Dave holds breath, cheeks puffed, gives thumbs-up, then panic.

NARRATOR: Dave doesn't have to worry about food, water, or monsters. Because Dave isn't going to last long enough to get hungry.

[V] Timer stops: **~10 SECONDS**. Tier board: Hadean → slot 12, red.

NARRATOR: Survival time: seconds. Next!

---

## #11 — THE ARCHEAN · 3.5 billion years ago (1:35–2:30)

[V] Cooled planet, shallow green-brown seas, bumpy rock mounds in the water (stromatolites), orange hazy sky.

NARRATOR: A billion years later, things look calmer. The ground has cooled, there are oceans, and there is life! …Sort of.

[V] Zoom on stromatolites. Dave pokes one.

NARRATOR: These lumpy rocks are stromatolites: layers built up by colonies of microbes. They are the most advanced life on the entire planet. And some of them are slowly doing something amazing: making oxygen.

NARRATOR: The problem is "slowly". In the Archean, the air has almost no free oxygen at all. Breathing it is like breathing pure nitrogen: you don't even feel like you're suffocating. You just… switch off.

[V] Dave yawns, sits down, lights dim.

NARRATOR: Dave passes out in under a minute. Survival time: about two.

[V] Timer: **~2 MINUTES**. Board: slot 11.

---

## #10 — SNOWBALL EARTH · 650 million years ago (2:30–3:25)

[V] Endless white ice to the horizon, pale sun. Dave lands face-first into snow.

NARRATOR: Good news, Dave: there's finally enough oxygen to breathe. Bad news: welcome to Snowball Earth.

NARRATOR: Around 700 to 635 million years ago, ice sheets may have spread close to the equator. Scientists still argue about how complete the freeze was, but much of the planet was buried under ice.

NARRATOR: There are no plants on land. No animals on land. Nothing to burn, nothing to eat, nothing to build a shelter with except ice.

[V] Dave builds a sad igloo; it collapses. Phone battery icon drops in the cold: 72% → 9%.

NARRATOR: Dave's phone dies first. Dave follows a few hours later. Hypothermia doesn't negotiate.

[V] Timer: **~6 HOURS**. Board: slot 10.

---

## #9 — THE GREAT DYING · 252 million years ago (3:25–4:25)

[V] Dark sky, lava fields on the horizon, dead grey forests, acid-looking green sea.

NARRATOR: Number nine is the worst disaster in the history of life.

NARRATOR: At the end of the Permian period, enormous volcanic eruptions in what is now Siberia pumped huge amounts of carbon dioxide into the air. The planet overheated, the oceans lost their oxygen, and life collapsed. Scientists estimate that around 80 to 90 percent of marine species disappeared.

[V] Dave finds a dead fish, a dead tree, a skull. Gives up, sits.

NARRATOR: Dave can breathe here. But the world around him is dying. Water is scarce or toxic, the heat is brutal, and there is almost nothing left to eat.

NARRATOR: The animals that made it through, like the stubby, tusked Lystrosaurus, survived because they could dig burrows and eat almost anything.

[V] A Lystrosaurus waddles past Dave, unbothered, and walks into a burrow. Dave tries to follow, gets stuck.

NARRATOR: Dave cannot dig burrows. Survival time: a few days.

[V] Timer: **~3 DAYS**. Board: slot 9.

---

## #8 — IMPACT DAY · 66 million years ago (4:25–5:20)

[V] Beautiful Cretaceous sunset. A bright dot in the sky growing bigger.

NARRATOR: Now, normally the Cretaceous is a decent place to land. Normally. But Dave's time machine has a sense of humour.

NARRATOR: Sixty-six million years ago, an asteroid around ten kilometres wide hit what is now Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula. Near the impact, everything ends instantly. Far away, the danger is slower: debris rains back down and heats the sky, wildfires break out, and soot and dust block the sun for months to years.

[V] Sky goes dark. Plants wilt. Dave holds his phone flashlight up; it's the only light on Earth.

NARRATOR: Without sunlight, plants stop growing. When plants stop, everything that eats plants starves. And then everything that eats *those* animals starves.

NARRATOR: If Dave is lucky enough to land far from the crater, he still joins the dinosaurs on the most famous losing team in history. Survival time: maybe a couple of weeks.

[V] Timer: **~2 WEEKS**. Board: slot 8.

---

## #7 — THE CAMBRIAN · 510 million years ago (5:20–6:15)

[V] Bare rocky coast, no plants. In the water, a burst of colour: trilobites, Anomalocaris.

NARRATOR: Number seven. The Cambrian, when animal life exploded in the oceans.

NARRATOR: Under the waves, everything is new: armored trilobites, Anomalocaris with its grabbing front claws, creatures with five eyes. On land? Absolutely nothing. Not a single tree. Not even moss as we know it.

[V] Dave on the shore holding a trilobite like a sandwich, unsure.

NARRATOR: So every calorie Dave can find is in the sea. He can grab shellfish and small creatures in the shallows, but he can't be sure what's safe to eat, there's no wood for a fire, and no shade from the sun.

NARRATOR: And oxygen levels at the time are still debated. They may have been noticeably lower than today, which means Dave gets tired fast.

[V] Dave pants after three steps.

NARRATOR: Survival time: about a week, if the seafood doesn't get him first.

[V] Timer: **~1 WEEK**. Board: slot 7.

---

## #6 — THE EARLY TRIASSIC · 248 million years ago (6:15–7:00)

[V] Orange desert, cracked ground, heat shimmer. Dave's hoodie hood up, sweating buckets.

NARRATOR: The planet survived the Great Dying, but it didn't recover quickly. In the early Triassic, parts of the tropics were so hot that studies suggest sea surface temperatures may have reached around forty degrees Celsius. That's hot-tub temperature. For the entire ocean.

NARRATOR: Most of the land is joined in one supercontinent, Pangaea, with huge dry interiors. Food is scarce, shade is scarce, water is scarcer.

[V] Dave takes off hoodie. Narrator: "No." Dave puts it back on (running gag: the hoodie never comes off).

NARRATOR: Survival time: about a week, as long as Dave lands close to water.

[V] Timer: **~1 WEEK**. Board: slot 6.

---

## #5 — THE CARBONIFEROUS · 310 million years ago (7:00–8:00)

[V] Dense green swamp forest, giant ferns, a dragonfly the size of a hawk.

NARRATOR: Halfway there. Number five, and the first era where Dave might actually make it a while.

NARRATOR: The Carboniferous is a planet covered in swampy forests. And the air is incredible: oxygen may have reached more than thirty percent, compared to about twenty-one today.

[V] Dave takes a deep breath, sparkles around him, does a little dance.

NARRATOR: Dave feels amazing. But that oxygen comes with side effects.

[V] Meganeura buzzes past. Then Arthropleura, a millipede as long as a car, crawls by. Dave freezes.

NARRATOR: Insects and their relatives grew huge. Meganeura had wings about seventy centimetres across. Arthropleura, a millipede relative, could grow more than two and a half metres long. The good news: Arthropleura probably ate plants. The bad news: you'll still have nightmares.

NARRATOR: The real danger is fire. With that much oxygen, wildfires could spread fast. And swamps mean infections, parasites, and sinking mud.

NARRATOR: But there's water, there are fish and small animals, and finally there's wood to burn. Survival time: possibly a few months.

[V] Timer: **~3 MONTHS**. Board: slot 5.

---

## #4 — THE JURASSIC · 150 million years ago (8:00–8:50)

[V] Huge conifer forests, a herd of long-necked sauropods in the distance. Dave with binoculars, delighted.

NARRATOR: Number four: the age of giant dinosaurs. And honestly? It's not the dinosaurs that worry us most.

NARRATOR: Yes, predators like Allosaurus are real. But a big predator isn't waiting behind every tree, and most animals would probably avoid a strange new creature like Dave at first.

[V] An Allosaurus sniffs Dave, sneezes, walks away. Dave offended.

NARRATOR: The bigger problem is food. There's no grass, no wheat, almost none of the fruit we know. Every unknown leaf or berry could be dinner, or poison.

NARRATOR: Near a river, with fish and eggs, Dave could last weeks. One bad encounter, and it's over. Survival time: about a month.

[V] Timer: **~1 MONTH**. Board: slot 4.

---

## #3 — THE LATE CRETACEOUS · 70 million years ago (8:50–9:45)

[V] Flowering plants, magnolias, a river delta. Distant silhouette of a Tyrannosaurus.

NARRATOR: Number three. The last chapter of the dinosaurs, a few million years before the asteroid. And it's surprisingly liveable.

NARRATOR: Flowering plants have spread, so there are more seeds and early fruits. Rivers are full of fish. The climate in many places is warm.

NARRATOR: And here's a myth to bust: Tyrannosaurus rex, Spinosaurus, and Velociraptor never lived together. They lived millions of years and thousands of kilometres apart. The movies lied to you.

[V] Three dinosaurs posing together for a "photo"; a red X stamps across it.

NARRATOR: Dave's biggest enemies here aren't monsters. They're infection, bad water, and food poisoning. Survival time: several months, in the right spot.

[V] Timer: **~6 MONTHS**. Board: slot 3.

---

## #2 — THE ICE AGE · 20,000 years ago (9:45–10:45)

[V] Steppe with mammoths, cold wind, Dave in hoodie shivering. Distant smoke from a camp.

NARRATOR: Number two. The Ice Age. And you might be thinking: how is freezing to death number two?

NARRATOR: Because the Ice Age wasn't frozen everywhere. There were grasslands, forests and river valleys full of animals. Plants and animals Dave would mostly recognise. And something else.

[V] Zoom on the smoke. Humans by a fire.

NARRATOR: Other humans. People who know which plants are safe, how to make fire, how to sew clothes from hides, and how to hunt a mammoth as a team.

NARRATOR: Alone, Dave is in trouble: cave lions, wolves and the cold would all be dangerous. But if those hunters let him join? Dave might survive for years.

[V] Dave waves awkwardly; a hunter hands him a fur. Dave tries to pay with his phone.

NARRATOR: That's a big "if". Survival time: from days to a lifetime, depending entirely on whether the locals like you.

[V] Timer: **DAYS → ???** (flickering). Board: slot 2.

---

## #1 — THE MIOCENE · 15 million years ago (10:45–11:45)

[V] Warm sunny coastline, palm-like trees, shellfish on rocks, a river mouth. Dave on the beach with sunglasses.

NARRATOR: And the best era of Earth's history to be stranded in… is one most people have never heard of. The Miocene.

NARRATOR: Fifteen million years ago, the planet was warmer than today. Continents look roughly familiar, grasslands are spreading, and coasts and rivers are full of fish and shellfish. The plants and animals are close enough to modern ones that Dave can actually recognise food.

NARRATOR: There are still dangers: big cats, giant sharks like Megalodon in the sea, and the occasional very large bear-dog. So, Dave: maybe don't swim.

[V] Dave about to dive in. Megalodon fin passes. Dave slowly walks back.

NARRATOR: But on a quiet stretch of coast, with fresh water, shellfish and a fire, Dave could live for a very long time. Survival time: potentially years.

[V] Timer: **YEARS**. Board: slot 1, gold.

---

## ENDING (11:45–12:30)

[V] Full tier board on screen, worst to best. Dave asleep in a hammock.

NARRATOR: So here's the final ranking. The Hadean kills you in seconds. The Archean in minutes. Snowball Earth, the Great Dying and impact day take a little longer. And the warm Miocene coast might let you grow old.

NARRATOR: But notice what actually kills Dave in almost every era. It's not dinosaurs. It's not giant bugs. It's the air, the cold, the heat, bad water and hunger.

NARRATOR: Every human alive today exists because a long line of ancestors solved those problems, one generation at a time. Dave just found out how hard that really was.

[V] Time machine glitches again. Dave vanishes from the hammock. Phone: "No signal". A new era label flickers: "ANCIENT EGYPT?"

NARRATOR: And he's not going home just yet. Where should Dave land next? Tell us in the comments, and subscribe, because Dave really can't survive alone.

[SFX] End sting. End screen: next video + subscribe.
