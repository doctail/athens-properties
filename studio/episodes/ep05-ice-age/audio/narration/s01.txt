First, a myth. The Ice Age was not a frozen planet, and it wasn't one long freeze either. Twenty thousand years ago falls inside the Last Glacial Maximum, the coldest stretch of the last glacial period, from about twenty-six thousand to nineteen thousand years ago. Ice sheets covered about a quarter of the world's land, compared with around eleven percent today. The world was roughly eleven degrees Fahrenheit colder, and the sea sat about four hundred feet lower than it does now. But that leaves most of the land with no ice at all. Africa and most of Asia had no ice sheets, and neither did Dave's patch of Eastern Europe. It was cold, dry, windy grassland, with herds of grazing animals, mammoths included. And here's the twist. By the scientific definition, we're still in an ice age, because Greenland and Antarctica still carry ice sheets. We just live in a warm gap between the cold stretches.
