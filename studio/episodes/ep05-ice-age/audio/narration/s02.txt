Fix number one: clothes. Dave trusts his hoodie. A neighbor offers him a fur, and Dave waves it away. Then the wind arrives. On a steppe, a cotton hoodie is more of a polite suggestion. Ice Age people had fur, and they had sewing. Needles made of bone, with an eye for thread, turn up at Ice Age sites across Eurasia, the oldest about forty thousand years old. Archaeologists still argue whether they made tailored coats or finer details. The skins had to come from somewhere. In the Czech Republic, sites about twenty-five to thirty thousand years old have fox, wolf and wolverine bones with skinning marks, though those animals may also have been eaten. Even lice have an opinion. A DNA study of clothing lice suggests people were wearing clothes at least eighty thousand years ago, maybe much earlier. Dave's hoodie gets demoted to underwear.
