Fix number four: manners. In 1977, psychologist Irwin Altman argued that the need for privacy shows up in every culture, but the methods differ. People control contact with words, body language and personal space, not only with walls. In a camp with no walls, that may mean lowering your voice, turning your back, or simply being left alone because everyone knows the rule. Nobody can prove it for the Stone Age. It's an inference. The rules had a price. Among the Ju/'hoansi, about a third of daytime conversations were complaints, criticism and gossip, a way to keep everyone in line. When everyone can see you, reputation matters. At night the tone changed: stories, songs and dances, often about people everyone knew.
