First, a warning. The Stone Age lasted more than two and a half million years, and nobody kept a diary. So researchers combine what archaeology digs up with how living foragers, like the Hadza of Tanzania, organize their camps. That is an educated comparison, not proof. A study of thirty-two modern foraging societies found that a typical band had about twenty-eight adults, plus their children. Dozens of people, one small camp. And in many camps most of them weren't even close relatives. Imagine sharing a bedroom with thirty near strangers. Among the Ju/'hoansi of the Kalahari, anthropologist Patricia Draper described camps so crowded that neighbors could see and hear most of what everyone else was doing. Yet she found no physical signs that the crowding was stressing people out. Remember that. It's a clue. Dave's first fix is a modern one: a Do Not Disturb sign. Nobody reads it. Writing is still thousands of years away. A toddler eats it. Dave decides he needs something sturdier. Like a wall.
