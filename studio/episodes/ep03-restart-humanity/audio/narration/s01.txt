Day one. Carla catches a fish. Dave makes a plan. And the first question isn't whether they can survive. It's whether they can have children, and that comes down to one number: how many people you need to keep a species going. Dave's math is simple. Two people, plus two people, equals a family. Right? Conservation biologists use a rule of thumb for wild animals: a small population needs about fifty individuals to avoid rapid inbreeding, and about five hundred to keep its genetic diversity over the long run. Humans aren't a perfect match, but the logic is the same. Two people is far below both numbers. Dave gives a thumbs up anyway. Carla does not. Because the number is only the first problem. The second one is what happens to the children.
